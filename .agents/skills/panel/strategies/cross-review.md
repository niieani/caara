# Cross-review

Dominant risk: **self-bias** — work produced by one mind reviewed by that same mind misses what it
missed the first time. A different model family reviews; the producer answers.

The work may pre-exist (your session's own diff, a document from elsewhere) or be produced by a
producer seat in this run. Either way the reviewer must come from a different model family than
the work's author.

## Seats

| Seat | Mode | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| producer | warm | absent when the work pre-exists; then the response round is yours at adjudication |
| reviewer | cold-briefed | gets the work product only — never the producer's reasoning history or chat |
| fresh juror | cold-briefed, on deadlock | a third family, roster permitting |

## Flow

1. **Review.** The reviewer's required reading is the work product itself (artifact paths, or the
   diff ref). Findings artifact: each finding is a claim plus evidence plus severity, and any
   cheaply checkable claim must be verified before it is written (run the test, run the repro) —
   a finding that could have been checked and wasn't is an opinion.
2. **Probe blanket approval.** "No findings" or unqualified approval within the first turn gets
   the [anti-laziness probe](../panelist-contract.md) before you accept it.
3. **Respond.** The producer (warm) reads the findings artifact and answers each finding:
   accept-with-cause and fix, or rebut with evidence. Blanket acceptance of all findings is
   capitulation — flag and probe, same as blanket approval.
4. **Break deadlocks.** A finding still contested after one rebuttal exchange goes to the fresh
   juror, ruling from the artifacts.
5. **Synthesize and adjudicate.** Cross-review runs are usually terminal with small inputs, so the
   seating tests typically put synthesis inline with you — this is the sanctioned-read case. With
   no producer seat, the response round is also yours: act on each finding at adjudication,
   accept-with-cause or rebut-with-evidence, same vocabulary.

## Convergence point

Every finding is accepted-and-fixed, rebutted-with-evidence, or ruled on by the juror.
